## 2. What actually holds

Protocol prediction 1 confirmed: latest-wins is perfect on "what does it say
now" and **scores zero** on "what did it say at T". That is not a defect in arm
A; it is what ignoring the question's instant means, and it is the gap the
temporal axis exists to fill.

Protocol prediction 3 confirmed on this sample: arm C matches A on type-1 and is
1.00 where A is 0.00 on type-2. Prediction 5's withdrawal condition — *if C's
as-of accuracy is not clearly above A's, the claim must be withdrawn* — is not
triggered.

Replay fidelity at 1.00 is the number the claim actually rests on, and it is the
one a temporal database does not give for free: not the row value, but the
supporting fact, reproducible by hash at the past instant.

## 3. Three things this does not show, stated before anyone reads it as more

**Arm B produced no result.** All 11 of its comparisons were exact lexical ties:
the query is a section heading and both candidates are that same section's text,
so overlap has nothing to discriminate on. Its 0.00/1.00 is the fixed tie-break
(earlier candidate wins) and nothing else. **No claim may be made that TAVONEL
beat a retrieval baseline on this data.** Arm B did not supply one, and its
numbers looking favourable makes reporting that more important, not less.

**Arm C's 1.00/1.00 is narrower than it looks.** The resolver is handed the two
values already carrying correct valid-time windows. It shows the resolver *uses*
valid time correctly. It does not show the system can *extract* valid time from
a document, which is the harder half and is untested here.

**Type-3 is untested, not confirmed.** A linear revision chain never has two
claims live in overlapping valid-time windows, so the corpus cannot produce a
conflict question. Protocol prediction 4 — that arms A and B show a
false-confidence rate of 1.0 — therefore has no evidence either way.
Constructing overlapping windows would have been inventing the very conflict arm
C is credited for handling, so none were constructed.

## 4. Limitations carried from the protocol, unchanged

- **11 questions.** Units whose text did not change carry no question, and units
  present in only one revision are excluded because ground truth would be
  ambiguous. No interval is reported for a sample this thin.
- **Known time is uniform.** Everything was acquired at one cutoff, so only the
  valid-time axis is exercised. Bi-temporality is half-tested at best.
- **Not legal or regulatory documents.** Nothing here supports a claim about
  contracts, regulations or policy documents.
- **Bi-temporality remains anticipated art.** SQL:2011 standardised
  system-versioned and application-time tables. This measures a combination, and
  no measurement makes the underlying idea novel.

## 5. One thing the run found in the implementation

The first attempt scored replay fidelity **0.00**, and the cause was correct
behaviour: `TemporalFact.temporal_source` defaults to `UNKNOWN`, and an as-of
query under `EXCLUDE_UNKNOWN` drops a fact whose validity dates have no stated
origin. The experiment had supplied dates without saying where they came from.
Declaring them `EXPLICIT` — the MediaWiki revision timestamp, which the document
does state — is what the data supports, and fidelity went to 1.00.

Worth recording because the failure looked like a bug in the system and was a
bug in the experiment. A version that "fixed" it by relaxing the policy would
have produced the same 1.00 while destroying the guarantee.
