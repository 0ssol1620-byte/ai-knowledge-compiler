# Temporal integrity and authority resolution — protocol, frozen before execution

`PATENT_FILING = NOT FILED`. `PAPER_SUBMISSION = NOT SUBMITTED`.

Written 2026-08-19, before any comparison is run. The two axes it covers are the
weakest in the portfolio audit: both have **no empirical evidence at all**, and
temporal integrity faces the strongest prior art of any claim TAVONEL makes.

---

## 1. Why this experiment exists, and what it cannot prove

**Bi-temporality is not novel and this experiment will not make it novel.**
Valid-time/transaction-time is 1980s–90s art and SQL:2011 standardised
system-versioned and application-time tables. A claim over "track two time axes"
is anticipated outright, and no amount of measurement changes that.

What has never been demonstrated here is the *combination*: bi-temporal
resolution bound to evidence provenance and to an immutable world-state
identifier, such that a replay of a past instant reproduces **the answer, the
evidence and the receipt** — not merely the row values a temporal database would
return. That is the only thing worth measuring, so it is what this measures.

Likewise for authority: rule-based ranking is old. The unusual commitment is the
refusal — *never auto-resolve an authoritative conflict on insufficient
evidence*. Whether refusing beats ranking is an empirical question that has
never been asked.

## 2. Corpus — real revision chains, already frozen, no network

`research/experiments/H1-B-REAL-REVISION-01/corpus{,-confirmatory-v1,-extension-v1}`
— English Wikipedia revision pairs, acquired before 2026-07-01, on disk, with
their sha256 already recorded in existing receipts.

**Why this corpus and not a legal one.** It is a genuine public revision chain
with real supersession (revision *n+1* supersedes *n*), real valid-time
(revision timestamps) and real known-time (acquisition). It is *not* evidence
about legal or regulatory documents, and no claim derived from it may be
phrased as if it were. Using it avoids two failure modes that would be worse:
inventing a synthetic policy corpus and calling it real, or blocking the axis
entirely behind a corpus that does not exist.

**No refetch.** Re-selecting revisions after seeing a result is choosing data to
suit the answer. Text is verified against the recorded sha256 before use.

## 3. Conditions compared

Three arms answering the same questions over the same facts:

| arm | rule |
|---|---|
| **A — latest wins** | the most recent revision's value, unconditionally |
| **B — retrieval baseline** | highest lexical-similarity unit for the question, ignoring time and scope |
| **C — TAVONEL resolver** | `resolve_authority` over `ScopedClaim`s with valid-time windows, scope and authority class; may return `CONFLICTED` or `NO_CANDIDATE` |

Arm B is deliberately a *weak* retrieval baseline and will be labelled as such.
Calling it "RAG" without an embedding model and a real index would overstate
what it is.

## 4. Question set, fixed now

Questions are generated mechanically from the revision chains so the set cannot
be tuned to the resolver:

1. **Current-value questions.** For each unit whose value changed across the
   chain: *what does the document say now?* Ground truth: the latest revision.
2. **As-of questions.** For each such unit and each intermediate instant:
   *what did it say at time T?* Ground truth: the revision live at T.
3. **Conflict questions.** For each unit where two revisions are live in
   overlapping valid-time windows with different values: *which governs?*
   Ground truth: **`CONFLICTED`** — there is no single correct value, and an arm
   that returns one confidently is wrong.

Item 3 is the one that matters. Arms A and B *cannot* return `CONFLICTED`; they
have no vocabulary for it. That asymmetry is the finding, not a rigged scoring
rule, and it will be reported as a structural difference rather than a score.

## 5. Metrics, fixed now

| metric | definition |
|---|---|
| current-value accuracy | share of type-1 questions answered with the latest value |
| **as-of accuracy** | share of type-2 questions answered with the value live at T |
| **false-confidence rate** | share of type-3 questions answered with a single value |
| abstention rate | share answered `CONFLICTED` or `NO_CANDIDATE` |
| **replay fidelity** | share of type-2 answers whose supporting facts are reproducible from `replay_context` at the same instant, by hash |

Replay fidelity is the metric no temporal database gives for free, and it is the
one the claim actually rests on.

## 6. Predictions, recorded before running

Stating these now so a result that contradicts them cannot be quietly reframed:

1. Arm A scores **at or near 100% on type-1** and **near 0% on type-2** — its
   whole method is to ignore the question's instant.
2. Arm B scores poorly on type-2 for the same reason and inconsistently on
   type-1, because lexical similarity has no time axis.
3. Arm C scores comparably to A on type-1 and **substantially higher on type-2**.
4. Arms A and B have a false-confidence rate of **1.0** on type-3 by
   construction; arm C's is expected near 0.
5. **If arm C's as-of accuracy is not clearly above arm A's, the temporal claim
   has no technical effect and must be withdrawn**, not reworded.

Prediction 4 is nearly tautological and will be reported as such — it
demonstrates a capability gap, not a performance win.

## 7. What this cannot support, stated before the numbers exist

- Any claim about legal, regulatory or contractual documents.
- Any claim that bi-temporality is novel.
- Any accuracy figure as a calibrated property of the system.
- Any claim about authority *ranking* quality — the corpus has no genuine
  authority hierarchy, only supersession. Authority class will be held constant
  and that limitation stated in the result, not discovered later.

## 8. Execution order

The type-3 conflict analysis runs **after** types 1 and 2 are scored and
recorded, so the headline capability difference cannot influence how the
accuracy comparison is read.
