# H1-K Amendment 2 — the 0.80 ceiling is sound but not an exactness lemma

**P2-13 §5 asked whether `PRUNED_CANDIDATE_SCORE_CEILING = 0.80` can be reused as
the pruning proof for an exact sparse matcher. It cannot.**

Evidence: `receipts/pruning-ceiling-lemma-audit-2026-08-19.json`.
`identity.py` was not modified; pruning is simulated by withholding the candidate.

---

## The lemma is correct, and correctly scoped

Its comment claims exactly one consequence:

> "Pruning therefore cannot turn AMBIGUOUS or NEW into MATCHED for a given row."

That is sound. `0.80 < 0.92` so a pruned candidate cannot itself merge, and
`0.92 − 0.05 = 0.87 > 0.80` so it cannot be the runner-up that fires the tie
guard on a pair that would otherwise merge. The comment is also already explicit
that it does not prove whole-assignment equivalence.

**The lemma was never written as an exactness lemma.** Reusing it as one was the
proposal under audit, and that is what fails.

## Gap (a) — AMBIGUOUS → NEW, measured

**8 of 8 constructed cases flip.** Every candidate has disjoint path roots, so
`_path_agreement = 0.0` and the ceiling licenses pruning it:

| kept | withheld |
|---|---|
| `ambiguous`, `logical_id=None` | `new`, `logical_id=n1` |

Both outcomes are non-MATCHED, so **the lemma's claim survives intact while the
classification changes.** That is precisely the difference between "cannot
promote to MATCHED" and "cannot change the answer".

### The mechanism is not the one predicted, and the finding is stronger for it

The audit predicted this via the review band: a pruned candidate scoring in
`[0.75, 0.80]` would be AMBIGUOUS when kept and NEW when withheld.

**Observed scores are 0.60 and 0.4286 — below the 0.75 floor.** The AMBIGUOUS
comes from the *missing-critical-signal* branch, which is evaluated before the
threshold check and returns AMBIGUOUS at any score.

The prediction needed a narrow score window. The real mechanism needs none:
withholding any candidate that would have been a row's assigned partner can flip
AMBIGUOUS → NEW whenever a critical signal is absent, anywhere in the pruning
regime. The `[0.75, 0.80]` overlap remains a **separate, unexercised** analytic
gap and is reported as analysis, not measurement.

## Gap (b) — runner-up and candidate attribution, analytic

The lemma excludes a pruned candidate from firing the tie guard on a
*would-otherwise-merge* pair. It says nothing about a pair already in the review
band, where the tie guard returns `candidates=(best, runner_up)`. Withholding a
pruned candidate can change that tuple.

**Amendment 1 is what makes this load-bearing.** Before it, this looked cosmetic.
Now candidate attribution is known to seed selective recompilation and the
promotion gate and to be serialized, so changing it changes the rebuild set.

Not measured here, and labelled `"measured": false` in the receipt.

## Why AMBIGUOUS → NEW is the serious direction

AMBIGUOUS carries `logical_id=None`. NEW carries a fresh seed id. So this flip
does not merely relabel a row — it decides whether the next revision continues an
identity or starts one. **It is the same lineage-fork mechanism that turned H1-E's
8 residual divergences into H1-E2's 8/8 future-path divergences.**

## Consequence for P2-13, binding on the protocol not yet frozen

An exact pruning proof must establish more than the ceiling does:

1. the pruned edge cannot be the **assigned partner** of any row — not merely
   cannot be a MATCHED partner;
2. the pruned edge cannot alter the **runner-up** of any row;
3. and therefore cannot alter the candidate attribution.

**Preserving the winner is not sufficient. The classification-relevant
alternative set must be preserved too** — which is exactly what P2-13 §4.1
anticipated and what this audit now confirms with a measurement.

The existing ceiling remains valid for what it is used for today: the BLOCKED
shadow policy's per-row fail-closed argument. It is not promoted, and this
amendment does not weaken it — it declines to reuse it for a stronger purpose.
