# Tables — paper package

Every table below separates the **statistical unit** from the **repetition
count**. A generated corpus repeated 500 times is 500 repetitions of one
constructed cell, not 500 independent samples of the world, and presenting it as
the latter is the commonest way an evidence base is silently inflated.

Column meanings, fixed for every table:

- `n_units` — independent constructed or natural units. This is the number that
  bounds what can be generalised.
- `n_repetitions` — executions per unit. Bounds only variance, never scope.
- `evidence_class` — one of `controlled`, `real-retrospective`,
  `contract-audit`, `blocked`, `not-run`.

## Table 3 — Identity matcher comparison

The central table of the identity story.

| policy | semantic exactness | future-path exactness | candidate attribution | runtime vs dense | promotion status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `LEGACY` (dense global assignment) | exact by definition | exact by definition | exact by definition | 1.0× (baseline) | **in production** |
| `BLOCKED` (candidate-indexed buckets) | **8 divergences / 33,600 decisions** | **0/8 preserved** — all 8 diverge next revision | not preserved | ~47× faster at 1,000 units | **shadow only — promotion veto** |
| `CERTIFIED_SPARSE` (admissible bound + certificate) | **0 divergences** (50 comparisons + 70 topology comparisons) | **0 divergences at revision 2** (19 cases, measured) | **preserved** | **0.35–0.48×**, i.e. slower | **not proposed for promotion** |

**The two failures are different in kind.** `BLOCKED` is fast and produces a
different history. `CERTIFIED_SPARSE` produces the right history and is slower
than the thing it was meant to accelerate.

**Why `CERTIFIED_SPARSE` is slower — structural, not an optimisation defect.**
Preserving the runner-up requires retaining `min(M, N+1)` candidates per unit,
because up to `N−1` prior units are claimed by other units. When two revisions
have similar unit counts, `N+1 > M` and every candidate is retained.

**A note on how this table was checked.** Its future-path column originally read
"preserved on tested corpus" while nothing had measured next-revision lineage —
the exactness harness stopped at revision 1. That was an overclaim, found in our
own second red-team pass and closed by running the measurement rather than
softening the wording.

That pass then found three further contract facets uncompared, and the sentence
announcing all twelve covered turned out to enumerate **eleven**: the assigned
column, the one per-row facet the comparison function never read, was absent and
had been called "indirectly implied" by the logical identifier. It is not — the
identifier is read from the matched partner, so two prior units sharing one are
indistinguishable through it. Measured directly by observing the resolver's own
entry points: **0 divergences over 19 cases**, with instrument liveness recorded
beside the null (72 observations, 67 assigned columns). All twelve facets are now
compared directly.

**How many results this row is.** One. The exactness figure is a single
measurement over a single constructed corpus of 19 cases, examined along twelve
contract facets; the facet extensions are additional *comparisons* over the same
corpus, not additional corpora. Adding facets can only reveal divergence and never
hide it, so they strengthen the conclusion — but they do not widen the population
it holds over, and two of the 19 cases were added after the first runs failed to
separate. The 70 H1-E topology comparisons are the one corpus-independent arm,
drawn from a different generator at a pinned seed, and are reported separately for
that reason.

**The `min(M, N+1)` account is post-hoc.** It is a derivation from the matching
formulation rather than a pre-registered prediction, and it was written after the
timing result it explains. It is offered as a structural explanation, not as a
tested hypothesis.

**Not established.** That exact acceleration is impossible; that a maximum-weight
assignment is theoretically required; that no faster exact algorithm exists. What
was tested is one certified strategy, and it did not accelerate.

**A correction that belongs in this table.** The W6 row previously read "v5
acquisition worker died". That cause was inferred from an on-disk signature — one
pair directory, no completion sidecar — and never verified. A second run
reproduced the signature byte-for-byte and produced a traceback: a deterministic
`ValueError` raised while computing a path for the receipt, on the first eligible
page of every run. The blocker was a call-site defect, not an unreliable worker
and not an external resource. It is stated here because the wrong cause had been
carried in four documents, and because "the worker died" is the kind of
explanation that closes an investigation instead of opening one.

**None of these is presented as a pending success.** Each is a state with a
contract attached, and three of the five cannot be discharged from this
environment at all.
