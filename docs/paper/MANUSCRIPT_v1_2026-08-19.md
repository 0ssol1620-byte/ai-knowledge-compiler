## Abstract

Systems that supply organizational knowledge to language models and agents have
no mechanism for telling a consumer that what it retrieved has since become
false. When a source document changes, derived artifacts — summaries, extracted
obligations, aggregates — become stale silently, and retrieval returns them with
undiminished confidence. Recompiling everything on every change is too
expensive; recompiling incrementally is unsafe, because the artifact a change
*should* have invalidated is the one most likely to be missed.

We describe a knowledge compiler that treats this as a build-integrity problem
rather than a retrieval problem: source changes are classified into typed change
kinds, propagated along dependency edges typed by change kind, and used to
recompile a *candidate* knowledge state which is activated in a single
state-transition event, and only after a verification gate passes. We report a pre-registered one-shot
controlled holdout in which selective recompilation was artifact-exact
equivalent to an independent full rebuild across nine adversarial topologies with
zero stale artifacts surviving. A retrospective all-stored regression over 28
natural English-Wikipedia revision pairs also matched the full-rebuild oracle in
all 28 pairs with zero stale artifacts left behind; 11 pairs contained semantic
changes under the evaluation adapter. Separately, 22 constructed authority,
bi-temporal and agent-consumption-lineage cells were each repeated 500 times and
all passed — a result we then qualified by ablating the governance filters one at
a time, which found six essential, three redundant-but-load-bearing (masked
whenever a compliant alternative exists, and the sole refusal when none does),
and four not independently measurable by that instrument. We report a
pre-registered confirmatory result on a frozen 800-page real-document holdout in
which a two-signal selective-acceptance gate was associated with severe-error
risk, and we state plainly that its effect magnitude remains uncertain.

We also report what did not work. A structure-only change once left a stale
artifact marked current in our own system; that failure is reproduced here
because it is the reason one of our mechanisms exists. Our first
same-intelligence evaluation was invalidated by leakage and its replacement has
not been run: its corpus has since been acquired and verified, and the question
set, the leakage measurement and the arms have not. And the semantic identity stage that
produces the change set exposed a scalability bottleneck: a sparse shadow matcher
removed the measured runtime/memory ceiling, but its eight residual divergences
were each a `NEW`/`AMBIGUOUS` classification flip, and all eight were measured
path-dependent into the following revision. `AMBIGUOUS` carries no logical
identifier, so a flip forks lineage rather than resolving, and that faster
matcher was not promoted. We report the performance gain and the promotion veto separately rather
than using one to erase the other.

We then built an exact replacement that preserves the full observable matching
contract — classification, logical identity, candidate attribution, runner-up and
downstream impact — and verified it against mutants that corrupt each of those
facets. It is exact and it is two to three times *slower* than the dense baseline
it was meant to accelerate. The obstruction is the runner-up rather than the
winner: because up to N−1 prior units are claimed by other units, a unit's rival
can be its (N+1)-th best candidate, so preserving that facet retains essentially
every candidate whenever two revisions have similar unit counts. Exact sparse
acceleration of history-sensitive assignment remains open, and we report which
facet closes the door. Two claim limitations were also narrowed by their own
experiments: a verification-gate ordering limitation that a permutation study
could not support, and a ranking element we found provably redundant with
another.

## 1. Introduction

Document-processing systems are usually evaluated as if documents were static:
a file arrives, a pipeline extracts structure, and quality is the fidelity of
that extraction. Organizational knowledge does not behave that way. Contracts are
amended, policies are superseded, specifications are revised, and the artifacts
an agent consumes are built from many documents at once. The interesting failure
is therefore not a mis-read character. It is an answer that remains confidently
available after the document it rests on has changed.

This turns document processing into a compilation problem over a mutable world.
A revision arrives; some semantic units continue their prior identity and some do
not; artifacts that depended on the changed units become stale; the rest may be
carried forward; and the resulting candidate state may be activated only if it
can be shown to contain no stale artifact. Each of those steps is a place where a
system can look successful and be wrong.

Three properties make the problem harder than incremental build systems, which
solved a superficially similar problem decades ago. First, **identity is not
given**: a build system is told which file changed, whereas a revised document
must have its units matched to their predecessors, and that matching can be
ambiguous. Second, **the dependency edges are typed by change kind**: a
reordering that alters no text still invalidates artifacts aggregated over
document position, and a formulation that seeds invalidation only from content
changes will silently carry those forward — we measured exactly this failure.
Third, **abstention is a required outcome**: where identity cannot be settled,
the honest result is an unresolved marker that forces a rebuild, not a confident
guess that rewrites a history.

This paper reports what happens when those properties are taken seriously and
then measured rather than asserted. Several of our results are negative, and they
are the ones we consider most useful: a faster identity policy that we vetoed, an
exact replacement for it that turned out slower than the baseline it was meant to
accelerate, and a claim limitation we withdrew after a permutation study failed to
support it.

**A note on what this system is not.** It is not a better OCR engine and not a
retrieval-augmented generation pipeline. Extraction quality and retrieval are
inputs to the problem studied here, not the problem. Nothing in this paper claims
an improvement to either.

**Contributions.**

1. A change-kind-typed dependency formulation in which a change that alters only
   document *structure*, producing no content-level change, still seeds
   invalidation — motivated by a measured failure of the naive formulation.
2. A pre-activation verification gate — a conjunction of candidate-state
   predicates whose acceptance result we measured to be invariant to evaluation
   order — under which an artifact whose input fingerprint cannot be verified is
   treated as *unverifiable rather than passing*, and a candidate state
   containing one is not activated.
3. A pre-registered one-shot controlled holdout establishing artifact-exact
   equivalence between selective recompilation and independent full rebuild.
4. A retrospective real-corpus regression over all 28 already-stored natural
   Wikipedia revision pairs, reported explicitly as non-confirmatory and
   single-source-family evidence.
5. A characterisation of where the approach's cost actually sits, including a
   scalability bottleneck we did not initially expect and do not conceal.
6. A set of negative and invalidated results retained rather than discarded,
   including a faster identity policy that failed a later-revision invariance
   criterion and therefore remained shadow-only.
7. An exact-semantics replacement for that policy, verified against mutants
   corrupting each facet of the matching contract, which is **slower** than the
   dense baseline it was built to accelerate — together with a post-hoc structural
   account, that preserving the runner-up rather than the winner is what removed
   the sparsity advantage **under this certification strategy**.
8. Two claim limitations narrowed by experiments designed to test them: a
   verification-gate ordering limitation that a permutation study could not
   support, and a ranking element found provably redundant with another.

Contributions 5 through 8 are negative or narrowing results. We list them as
contributions rather than as caveats because each changed a decision — one vetoed
a promotion, one closed a design direction, and two removed claim language.

## 1.1 Related work

**Incremental and selective rebuild.** Dependency-driven partial rebuild is old
and well understood, from `make` [1] through modern build systems organised
around content addressing [2, 3], and through data-lineage tooling in the
analytics world [4, 5]. We claim no
novelty in incremental rebuild as such. What differs here is that the dependency
edges must be *typed by the kind of change* that traversed them, and that the
nodes' identities are inferred rather than declared. A build system is told that
`a.c` changed; we must first decide whether this revision's paragraph is the same
paragraph as last revision's.

**Entity resolution and record linkage.** Matching units across revisions is a
one-to-one assignment problem, and maximum-weight bipartite matching is the
standard tool [6, 7]. The record-linkage literature [8, 9] focuses on match
quality; our constraint is
different and stricter. Because a match decides which *history* continues, an
assignment that is equally good by score but different in attachment is not
equivalent for our purposes — it forks a lineage. Section 4.9 reports what that
constraint costs when one tries to accelerate the matcher.

**Bitemporal data and as-of queries.** Valid-time and transaction-time modelling
is prior art [10, 11] and we use it as such. It appears here only composed with authority
ordering and permission filtering, and §4.6 reports an ablation showing which of
those filters are individually load-bearing and which are redundant-but-still-
load-bearing when no compliant alternative exists.

**Retrieval-augmented generation.** RAG [12] addresses which passages to place
in a context window. It does not address whether the corpus behind those passages is
currently consistent, nor what must be rebuilt when it is not. The two are
complementary, and we make no comparative claim against retrieval systems.

**Transactional publication.** Our activation step is an *ordered refusal
followed by a single-event switch*, and the property a consumer observes is that
no intermediate state is visible [13, 14]. It is not a distributed transaction
protocol
and we do not evaluate crash behaviour, partial-failure recovery or multi-node
consensus. §4.7 states the boundary explicitly because it is the easiest of our
results to over-read.

## 2. Method

Figure 1 shows the whole path a revision takes, from source document to a
consumer's context window, and marks the two places the system is allowed to
refuse: the identity resolver's abstention and the activation gate. The four
subsections below take those stages in order.

### 2.1 Stable semantic identity

Each derived knowledge unit carries a logical identifier intended to survive
source revision. Resolution between versions is a one-to-one assignment: no
prior unit may be claimed as the continuation of two incoming units, because
independent per-unit resolution silently forks histories. Scoring is a weighted
mean over seven signals renormalised over the signals that *have values* — a
missing signal is recorded with a reason, never filled with zero — and when a
signal designated critical has no value the resolver abstains. Figure 4 gives
the outcome space of a single resolution — matched, new, or unresolved — and
shows that an unresolved unit contributes no traversal seed rather than a
default one.

The bands are deliberately conservative: continuation at ≥ 0.92, a reviewable
band down to 0.75, a new identity below that, and abstention when the two best
candidates fall within 0.05 of each other. The asymmetry is intentional. A false
merge rewrites the history of a clause and no later query can detect it; a false
split is visible and joinable. **These thresholds are not calibrated.** The
system's own calibration table refuses to report itself calibrated without a
named corpus, and we do not present them as measured optima.

### 2.2 Typed change classification and impact

Differences between versions are classified into semantic, structural, temporal
and authority change kinds. Dependency edges are typed by change kind, and a
derived artifact declares which kinds it is sensitive to. Impact is computed by
traversing, from the logical identifiers associated with each classified change
kind, only the edges typed to that kind.

The structural channel exists because of a failure, described in §4.4.

### 2.3 Verification before activation

Selective recompilation produces a *candidate* knowledge state. Before it can
become active, a gate checks that every classified change is accounted
for by the affected set; that every affected artifact was rebuilt; that each
carried-forward artifact's recorded input fingerprint — covering declared inputs
*and* structural facts — matches; and that no artifact whose fingerprint cannot
be verified is marked current. An artifact that declares a structural dependency
without declared structural coverage is unverifiable, not passing. Activation occurs in a single state
transition, conditional on all checks, with no partially activated state visible
to a consumer. Figure 3 draws the four checks in the order the implementation
evaluates them; §4.8 reports that the order is not load-bearing.

**What the build receipt does and does not establish.** The receipt seals
`artifact_id ↔ artifact_sha256 ↔ input_fingerprint ↔ build_action_id ↔
parent_world_state_id ↔ builder_version`. It establishes that what was declared
was what was built. It does **not** establish that the declaration was complete:
a wrong input set can be sealed perfectly. We state this explicitly because the
seal's cryptographic strength invites exactly the wrong inference.

### 2.4 Retrieval under world, time, authority and permission

Queries are answered against the active state under a composed filter: valid
time and known time, authority and applicability, and permission. A unit
excluded by the permission filter is excluded such that its identifier is not
revealed — an excluded-but-named unit is a leak.

## 3. Experimental design

### 3.1 Pre-registration and one-shot discipline

Protocols are frozen before data are seen, and thresholds are never widened
after a result. Where a protocol had to change, the change is recorded as a
numbered amendment stating what changed, why, and whether any criterion moved;
in every amendment reported here, no acceptance criterion moved.

The Family B holdout is **one-shot**. Its generator version, seed hash, success
criteria and code were frozen before it was run, it was run once, and it has not
been re-run or re-tuned. A further change to Family B requires a new seed and a
new protocol, not a second attempt at this one.

### 3.2 Controlled versus real

We distinguish three evidence classes throughout and never let them substitute
for one another:

| class | meaning | used for |
|---|---|---|
| controlled | generated corpus with known ground truth | mechanism correctness |
| real | external document or revision corpus | external validity |
| implementation | unit tests only | neither |

Controlled evidence establishes that a mechanism does what it claims on inputs
we constructed. It says nothing about how often those inputs occur in practice.
Synthetic revisions are used for capability testing and are never substituted
for real temporal evidence.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Selective recompilation equals full rebuild (controlled, confirmatory)

On a pre-registered one-shot fresh-seed holdout, selective recompilation was
artifact-exact equivalent to an independent full rebuild in every cell, with
zero stale artifacts surviving and zero escapes missed by the gate.

| cell | cases | equivalent | escaped artifacts |
|---|---|---|---|
| unchanged | 400 | 400 | 0 |
| semantic_only | 400 | 400 | 0 |
| structural_only | 400 | 400 | 0 |
| mixed | 400 | 400 | 0 |
| ambiguous | 400 | 400 | 0 |
| deep_chain_64 | 200 | 200 | 0 |
| cross_document_dependency | 200 | 200 | 0 |
| diamond_conflicting_channels_semantic | 200 | 200 | 0 |
| diamond_conflicting_channels_structural | 200 | 200 | 0 |

`gate_missed_escape_total = 0`; `primary_success = true`.

**Reported against ourselves:** the `semantic_only` and `ambiguous` cells
rebuilt more than strictly changed — 400 and 454 false invalidations
respectively. Over-rebuilding is the safe direction, but it is a cost and it is
not netted out of the saving reported in §4.3.

#### 4.1.1 Natural-revision regression (real corpus, retrospective)

We then ran the **current pinned implementation** over every natural revision
pair already stored across the Family B development, confirmatory-corpus and
extension stores: 28 English-Wikipedia pairs / 56 source files. This protocol was
frozen to include *all* stored pairs rather than selecting titles by outcome, but
the data had been used by earlier work, so this is a retrospective regression —
not a fresh holdout and not a confirmatory endpoint.

Selective state matched the independent full-rebuild oracle on **28/28 pairs**,
with **zero stale artifacts left behind**. Eleven pairs contained semantic
changed logical identifiers under the evaluation adapter; the other 17
normalized to no semantic change. The mean planned rebuild fraction across all
28 pairs was 7.94%.

This closes a narrower question than the controlled holdout: current code also
reproduces equivalence on the natural revision material already in the repository.
It does **not** establish general equivalence. English Wikipedia is one source
family, the dependency graph is an evaluation adapter, and SEC/DART/contracts/
scans and unconstrained production graphs remain outside this result.

### 4.2 Authority, temporal integrity and lineage (controlled)

Across **22 constructed mechanism cells**, each repeated 500 times (11,000
executions), every cell passed. The
resolver selected the correct authority, abstained where an override lacked
evidence, marked exactly-equal conflicts conflicted rather than choosing,
never revealed a permission-hidden claim identifier, reproduced as-of answers on
both temporal axes, and reconstructed consumption lineage to depth 64 at
precision and recall 1.0 including against decoys.

**The limit of this result, stated as the receipt states it:** controlled
mechanism correctness only; no automatic temporal extraction and no real-world
frequency or generalization claim. In particular, valid-time intervals are
supplied correctly by construction. *Reading* a validity interval off a document
is a different capability and is untested. This result must not be written as
"the system understands temporal validity from documents."

### 4.3 Cost, split into the two stages it actually has

Downstream of change detection, given an already-computed dirty set, selective
recompilation was **3.62× (1,000 units)** and **4.31× (10,000 units)** faster
than full rebuild, at a planned-rebuild fraction of ~1.1% and ~1.0%.

The identity and change-detection stage that *produces* that dirty set scales
far worse. Figure 5 puts the two stages side by side, which is the comparison a
single end-to-end speedup number would hide:

| units | identity + change detection, p50 |
|---|---|
| 100 | 97.9 ms |
| 250 | 1.20 s |
| 500 | 3.02 s |
| 750 | 6.01 s |
| 1,000 | 10.48 s |
| 10,000 | `MemoryError` |

**These must never be combined into one end-to-end speedup.** Doing so would
hide the dominant cost inside the incremental saving, and it is the first
objection a reviewer should raise. We raise it ourselves.

The `MemoryError` above is a historical measurement from before an exact memory
fix that changed the assignment matrix from `(float, dict, dict)` cells to floats
and recomputed signal detail only for assigned pairs. That fix moved the LEGACY
ceiling: subsequent probes completed 1,500 and 2,500 units, so the table must not
be read as the current 10,000-unit failure boundary.

A candidate-indexed `BLOCKED` shadow policy went much further in a separate
`assign_one_to_one` microbenchmark: 767 ms at 1,000 units versus 36.0 s for the
unrestricted matcher (46.97× in that specific measurement), and 12.9 s at 10,000
units with a 33 MB peak. Those timings are **not comparable** to the
identity-plus-change-detection table above and are not an end-to-end speedup.

More importantly, performance did not determine promotion. Across 33,600
controlled identity decisions the shadow policy produced zero measured
fail-open and zero fail-closed divergences but eight residual tied split
assignments. A follow-on protocol frozen before its future-state endpoint found
that **all 8/8** row-attached divergences produced a different next-revision
decision; the negative control produced zero differences and the positive
falsification arm separated. The current `BLOCKED` implementation is therefore
`PROMOTION_VETO_PATH_DEPENDENT` and LEGACY remains default. The speed result is
retained; exact Protected-Core equivalence is not claimed.

### 4.4 A negative result we caused: structure-only staleness

An earlier version classified a change that altered only the order of units as
producing no changed logical identifiers. The impact traversal therefore had an
empty seed, and a position-aggregated artifact — genuinely stale — was carried
forward as CURRENT. The promotion gate blocked the run.

We record two things about this that are easy to get wrong. First, **the block
is not a pass**: equivalence had already been violated by the time the gate
fired, and scoring the run as a success because the safety net held would be
scoring the wrong thing. Second, the system never published a wrong world state;
what happened is that the planner produced a false-negative classification and
the first version of the gate had a fingerprint blind spot. The structural
dependency channel and the extended fingerprint exist because of this. Figure 2
puts the defective path and the corrected one side by side; it is the clearest
statement of what the typed-dependency channel adds over a content-hash
incremental build.

### 4.5 Selective acceptance on real documents (confirmatory)

> The pre-registered primary test showed an association between the combined
> selective-acceptance gate and severe-error risk on the frozen holdout (Fisher
> exact p = .032), while the corrected risk-difference bootstrap interval
> included zero, so the magnitude of risk reduction remains uncertain.

749 of 800 holdout pages carried an official score. Accepted: 648 pages, 38
severe. Abstained: 101 pages, 12 severe. Bootstrap interval for the risk
difference: [−0.21 pp, +12.79 pp].

**Disclosure required by our own rules:** the first evaluator used for this
endpoint was non-deterministic. The result above is the corrected run. The
flawed run and the comparison between them are retained in the repository rather
than replaced. This is a post-unsealing correction and is disclosed as one.

**A cohort limitation, not a design choice:** no source-grounded arm was
possible, because the frozen cohort is scan-image centred and carries no
compatible native text layer to ground against.

### 4.6 Same intelligence, better world: NOT RUN

The primary endpoint of our comparison between raw text, basic retrieval and a
compiled world has **not been run**, and we report the reasons rather than a
weakened substitute.

The first question set was invalidated by leakage: Recall@1 of 0.9969 with a
same-document share of 1.0 meant the baseline was not a baseline. The second was
built correctly but produced zero revision-sensitive facts, because the
available 56-pair corpus consisted of consecutive revisions in which infobox
facts do not move. We declined both available escapes — lowering the threshold,
and manufacturing questions from prose diffs — and declared the endpoint not
run. A candidate manifest of 6,987 long-horizon titles was frozen *before* any
revision outcome was inspected, so no selection leakage has been introduced;
acquisition against it failed twice on API transport rather than on science.

**A third acquisition succeeded and was spent on development, deliberately.** It
returned 750 titles, and a protocol conflict was found in the run that used
them. Rather than publish that run as an endpoint, we sealed those 750 titles as
development data. They are excluded from the confirmatory candidate pool by a
fail-closed guard, leaving **6,237 untouched titles**. We report the guard's own
failure too: an earlier version read a key the seal does not carry, so it removed
nothing while reporting success. An empty development-title list is now a
refusal, because an inert guard is worse than no guard — it produces a receipt
saying the check passed.

**The confirmatory apparatus is built, frozen and gated; the endpoint is still
not run at the time of writing.** What is fixed before any holdout number exists:

- *The model is pinned by attestation, not by name.* Two runtimes serving the
  same name can differ in checkpoint, tokenizer, quantization and serving stack.
  The candidate registry's expected revision, the revision attested from the live
  runtime, and the revision in the config freeze are the same string, and the
  checkpoint's files were verified against the digests the model hub declares for
  that commit, with zero mismatches. An unattestable runtime fails the gate; there
  is no fallback to another model.
- *Raw text receives the whole document pair,* not a shared retrieval budget.
  Under a shared 3,000-token budget the development run measured raw text losing
  the answer to truncation on 0.5763 of questions — that is a context-window
  effect, and reporting it as a representation effect would have been the easiest
  way to overstate our result. Exceeding the real context window is recorded as an
  overflow outcome, never truncated away silently.
- *One arm-fairness metric is demoted rather than used.* The share of questions
  separable between the two revisions is 1.0 by construction of the admission
  rule. We declare it a generator integrity check and do not cite it as evidence
  that the task is hard.
- *The model's default reasoning preamble is disabled in every arm.* **What was
  measured,** on the live runtime and reproduced on a second one: with the default
  chat template this checkpoint had not reached an answer at our fixed 256-token
  limit, and with the preamble disabled the same runtime returned a bare value in
  six completion tokens and the abstention sentinel in seven. **What we inferred
  from that,** and state as inference: under the same budget an enabled preamble
  would truncate mid-reasoning in every arm, so the run would report the preamble
  rather than the four representations. The setting is applied identically to all
  four arms, which changes the shared model contract and not the variable under
  test, and it means our result may not be read as measuring this model with
  reasoning enabled.

The gate that stands in front of the holdout has 21 conditions, all currently
passing, and each one is falsified in the same execution to show it can refuse.

**The confirmatory run was executed on 2026-08-20 and stopped before its arms.**
The holdout was acquired, a question set was generated from it, three pre-arm
gates were measured, one refused, and no arm was run. We report that outcome
rather than the result we set out to obtain.

*Acquisition.* The holdout title list is the frozen candidate manifest with the
sealed development titles removed: 6,237 titles, overlap zero. A first
acquisition attempt spent two and a half hours in rate-limit backoff and then
died on a deterministic path error in its own invocation --- the same defect this
repository had already diagnosed once and recorded --- writing no receipt, no
question and no result. Its partial output is quarantined unexamined and was not
reused. The second attempt ran over the identical sealed manifest, seal and
cutoffs; its command line was read from the operating system and the failing
expression simulated for both attempts before it was allowed to continue. It
returned **391 eligible revision pairs and 359 named exclusions from 750 titles
consumed, with none unaccounted for**, and a corpus root hash over every verified
pair. Nine fail-closed integrity checks passed, including zero overlap with the
sealed development titles.

*Question set.* The frozen generator produced **2,548 questions: 185
revision-sensitive primary and 2,363 unchanged controls**, over 265 documents
with parsable infoboxes.

*Gates.* The structural taint gate passed --- the reference builder produced zero
tainted queries and all three mutants were caught at 477 of 477. The residual
leakage gate passed --- residual overlap median and 90th percentile both 0.0
against frozen ceilings of 0.05, with the injected positive control exceeding and
the clean control within. **The baseline validity gate refused.**

*The refusal, exactly.* Six of its seven criteria passed. One did not:

> The v8 development set passed the frozen tie-rate criterion (7.34%), whereas
> the untouched confirmatory holdout exceeded the predeclared 10% ceiling
> (19/166, 11.45%).

A tie at the top of the ranking is resolved by index order, so a tied question is
a ranking decision the retriever did not make. Because the rate is a ratio of
integers over 166 evaluated questions, the ceiling has an exact integer form: 16
ties is the largest passing count (9.64%), 17 already fails (10.24%). **The
holdout exceeded the largest admissible count by three questions.** We do not
present this as a marginal 1.45-percentage-point excess; the criterion is a
deterministic comparison over counts, and 19 > 16.

*The ceiling's provenance,* because it is the obvious place to attack this
result. The v7 corpus measured 10.38%, which is **above** 10% --- the historical
observation motivated having the criterion, and v7 would not have passed it. Ten
percent was adopted as a round operational-quality ceiling, deliberately stricter
than the observation rather than fitted to it. It was pinned in a hashed
configuration freeze at 02:50:43Z, **28 minutes before the holdout title manifest
existed** and six hours before the holdout question set. It was not relaxed after
the holdout was measured. We do not claim more than that: **10% is a predeclared
operational validity threshold, not a theoretically derived constant.**

*What this result is.* The confirmatory benchmark failed its pre-arm
retrieval-validity criterion, so no representation-comparison endpoint was run.
The comparison between raw text, basic retrieval, enriched retrieval and a
compiled world is **not measured** --- not lost, not tied, not shown to be
absent. No arm ran; no answer was ever generated by the model; the measured
inference cost of this endpoint is zero.

*One diagnostic does survive, and it is not about our system.* Under the frozen
baseline retrieval representation, the strongest superseded-revision unit
outscored the oracle current-revision unit for **64.46%** of evaluated primary
questions (median gap -0.0537). We label this a **baseline retrieval difficulty
observation**: it says temporal retrieval on this corpus is hard for the frozen
baseline. It is not evidence for our system, and no arm result exists that could
make it so.

*What we did not do.* We did not raise or remove the ceiling, regenerate the
question set with different admission or tie-breaking, delete questions, change
the retriever, run the arms anyway, or build a replacement benchmark. The
threshold was fixed before the cohort existed and the cohort has now been
measured against it; any adjustment made after seeing 11.45% would convert a
confirmatory run into a development one and consume the only untouched cohort
this programme has. Those titles are development data from this point forward.

We consider a not-run endpoint with an intact protocol more useful than a run
endpoint with a compromised one.

### 4.7 The activation gate refuses, enumerably (contract audit)

The verification-and-activation path of §2.3 was audited under a sealed
protocol: the live `world_state` and `build_receipt` modules were hashed and
pinned, a list of 21 required contract behaviours was fixed, and the suite was
executed. All 21 behaviours were present and 49 tests passed.

The behaviours are enumerated rather than summarised, because "the gate works"
is not a result. Publication is refused on: a checksum mismatch; an integrity
failure; a permission check that was not executed; a missing or failing
equivalence check; an unstaged object; a receipt whose seal has been broken or
whose fields were tampered with; a receipt bound to a prior world or to another
artifact; wrong artifact bytes; and moved inputs. A failed publish leaves the
previously ACTIVE state in place and emits **no** event; a successful publish
emits **exactly one** event, bound to the manifest hash. A rejected candidate
cannot be retried and the same candidate cannot be published twice. Rollback is
supported, and refused for unpublished or current states. A builder without
tracked capture cannot reach HIGH_INTEGRITY; the permissive path yields only
DEGRADED_UNTRACKED.

**What this is, stated exactly.** This is a controlled implementation and
unit-contract audit. It is *not* a demonstration of distributed atomicity. We
have not exercised a process crash mid-transaction, a partial database, network
or storage failure, or multi-node concurrent publication. "Atomic" here means
the conditional single-step activation of a verified candidate, not a
distributed transaction guarantee.

It also does not move the build-record trust boundary. A builder that never
records an input class cannot be detected by receipt binding alone; the only
guarantee available is fail-closed or degraded treatment of untracked builders.
Reporting tests-passing as an empirical result about production behaviour would
be exactly the promotion this paper's status vocabulary exists to prevent.

### 4.8 The gate's check ordering is commutative, and the claim was narrowed

The promotion gate evaluates four checks in a fixed order, and an earlier draft
described that order as part of what prevents a stale artifact from being
promoted. Nothing measured it.

We permuted all 24 orders of the four checks over six constructed candidate
states (144 evaluations), including the case where an ordering effect would be
most likely to appear: a state that is internally consistent **and** incomplete.
**No permutation changed the admit/refuse verdict.**

A null is only as good as the instrument's ability to have returned something
else, and ours initially could not. The first harness was voided by a fidelity
gate that requires the canonical order to reproduce the real gate's verdict and
its first failing step; it failed on a state the real gate refuses. The second
harness passed fidelity but implemented the four checks as pure functions of the
candidate state, so no permutation could have diverged whatever the truth was --
the same non-separating defect we had already recorded for an earlier
falsification arm. Only after adding a positive control, a deliberately
order-dependent gate on which the harness diverged for 12 of 24 orders, did the
null become interpretable.

The result is that the gate's acceptance decision is a conjunction of
candidate-state predicates and is **invariant to evaluation order**. We
initially wrote that the four checks are *mutually independent*, and that was an
overclaim: order-invariance does not entail independence, since a conjunction of
coupled predicates is still commutative. A static audit of the implementation
found a coupling the permutation study could not have detected -- where no
full-rebuild oracle is available, the integrity check's pass is discharged by the
staleness check that follows it, and the source says so. The checks are
order-invariant; they are not independent, and we report the weaker proposition
because it is the one the evidence carries.

The ordering determines which refusal is reported, not whether one occurs. We
narrowed the corresponding patent limitation accordingly, under a rule frozen
before the experiment ran.

### 4.9 Exclusivity is what makes identity expensive, and it does not prune

Our identity resolver assigns incoming units to prior units by a global
maximum-weight matching, so one prior unit cannot be claimed as the continuation
of two new ones. That exclusivity is the property that keeps histories from
forking silently, and §4.3 reports the cost: a dense N x M scoring pass.

We first pursued the obvious acceleration -- restrict each unit's candidates to a
structurally plausible bucket. It is fast (~47x at 1,000 units) and it is wrong:
8 residual decisions diverged, and all 8 propagated into the following revision
as lineage forks. It remains shadow-only under a promotion veto. Figure 7 traces how a
window-restricted matcher turns a contended assignment into a fork, and Figure 8
tabulates the resulting trade with the negative result kept in the table rather
than dropped from it.

We then asked *why*, and the mechanism is sharper than "approximation loses
accuracy". Under the global assignment, a unit that loses a contended prior unit
to another unit is assigned nothing and is classified NEW. Split the window --
which is what any decomposition does -- and the same unit wins that prior unit
locally and lands in the ambiguous band instead. Since an ambiguous decision
carries no logical id, the next revision seeds a fresh identity, and a single
flipped classification becomes a permanent fork. Exclusivity is a *global*
property, and locality destroys it.

Finally we built the exact version. It never removes a candidate from the
problem; it computes a cheap admissible upper bound and pays for an exact score
only where the bound cannot certify the pair irrelevant. It reproduces the full
observable contract with zero divergences across 50 comparisons under
candidate-order and row-order perturbation, verified against three mutants that
each corrupt one of those facets.

"Full" is meant literally and was checked. All twelve facets of the frozen
contract are compared directly and all are exact: classification, logical id,
assigned column, candidate attribution, relation, score, unresolved seeds,
dependency impact, next-revision lineage, the serialized change record, the
changed-identifier set, and the selective rebuild set. Four of those were
unmeasured until we audited our own contract against our own harness, and the
route to that list is itself reported in §5.1 -- including the fact that an
earlier version of this sentence claimed twelve while enumerating eleven.

**Twelve facets is not twelve results.** All of them are compared over the *same*
constructed corpus of nineteen cases, and two of those cases were added after the
first runs failed to reach the code paths under test. Adding facets to a
comparison can only reveal divergence and never conceal it, so each additional
facet strengthens the conclusion — but none of them widens the population it
holds over. The one arm that is corpus-independent is the replay against the
identity-scalability generator at a pinned seed, 70 comparisons with 0
divergences, and we report it separately rather than folding it in.

**It is 2-3x slower than the dense baseline.** The obstruction is the runner-up,
not the winner. Because up to N-1 prior units are taken by other rows, a unit's
runner-up can be its (N+1)-th best candidate, so preserving that facet requires
retaining min(M, N+1) candidates per unit -- every candidate, when the two
revisions have similar unit counts.

We also ran it against the bucketing policy's own adversarial topologies, driven
from the same generators at the same pinned seed, and it is exact on all seven
cells including the two -- split-and-merge and near-tie -- that produced the eight
divergences which vetoed the fast policy. So the exactness is not an artifact of a
gentle corpus: it holds precisely where the fast policy failed. It is still
slower.

We report this as a negative result, and state its scope precisely: **under the
tested certification strategy, preserving global assignment, candidate
attribution and runner-up semantics removes the expected sparsity advantage.**
That is a statement about one strategy on one corpus, not about the problem.
Exact sparse acceleration of history-sensitive assignment remains open; a
different certificate for the runner-up, or a formulation that does not require
preserving it, is untested here and is not excluded by anything we measured.

### 4.10 An abstention says which signal was missing, and why (contract audit)

§2.1's identity resolver refuses rather than guesses when a signal it treats as
critical has no value. A refusal that says only "ambiguous" is indistinguishable
from a bug, so the resolver records what it had. We audited that record under the
same sealed protocol as §4.7: the live `identity` module was hashed and pinned, a
list of 12 required contract behaviours was fixed, and the suite was executed.
All 12 were present and passed.

The recorded behaviours are, again, enumerated rather than summarised. An
abstention caused by an absent signal carries the identities of the signals that
*were* scored, the identities of those that were **not**, and for each absent one
a reason drawn from the four the implementation distinguishes: the model was
unavailable, the signal was not applicable to this pair, a resource limit was
reached, or the evaluation errored. The two maps are disjoint, so an absent
signal is never given a numeric value — a zero is indistinguishable from measured
disagreement, and the two demand different recoveries. The operator-facing reason
names the absent critical signal. The whole record survives serialization.

**Two controls decide whether that means anything.** The positive control is a
pair on which every signal has an input: it must produce an empty absence map and
score all seven weighted signals, or the recital would be satisfied vacuously by
reporting absence unconditionally. The break controls construct the two defects
the contract forbids — a signal recorded as absent *and* scored, and a free-text
absence reason — and require the same predicates the passing tests rely on to
reject both. A guard that cannot fail is not a guard, and this programme has
found that defect in its own detectors often enough to test for it by default.

**What this is, stated exactly.** Controlled implementation evidence over the
live module. It establishes what the implementation records when a signal has no
value. It does **not** measure how often absence occurs in real documents, and it
must not be promoted to a real-data result. It also asserts no completeness: the
four reasons are what this implementation distinguishes, and the receipt records
`absence_taxonomy_completeness_claimed: false`. The corresponding patent claim
was amended the same day so that it recites an *enumerated reason distinguished
by the implementation* rather than "the reason each absent signal had no value",
which would have read onto a complete taxonomy nobody has.

Finally, "distinguishable from a failure to evaluate" is established in the
narrow sense the tests exercise. An abstention carrying an absence record is
distinguishable from a *tie* abstention, which is reached after a complete
evaluation and therefore carries an empty absence map; and an evaluation that is
refused outright produces no decision object at all, so there is nothing to
mistake for either. This is not a crash-safety or partial-failure result.

### 4.11 What the element-support pass changed about the claims

The evidence in this paper is also the support for a patent application, and the
two are audited against each other element by element rather than claim by claim,
because an examiner objects to a limitation and not to a claim. That audit
reported eight limitations whose recited scope reached past the receipt beneath
it. We record here what was done about them, because the alternative — quietly
leaving the broader wording in place — is the failure mode this whole programme
is built to avoid.

Six were closed by **amending the recital down to the measurement**. The
selective-acceptance holdout of §4.5 records attribution at page granularity and
compares per-page critical-token correspondence, so the two independent-claim
limitations that had recited *per-unit evidence regions* now recite what the
receipt contains. The change-classification limitation had listed four change
kinds in one recital, and only two of them — semantic and structural — are
exercised over the real revision corpus of §4.1; the other two are exercised over
constructed cells, and now sit in a dependent claim at that class. The
dependency-graph limitation had recited that a derived artifact authors its own
sensitivity declaration, which no receipt isolates from the typed graph itself.
The identity limitation had bound the measured half — identifier persistence
across revisions — to an unmeasured half in the same sentence. And the
unresolved-identity limitation had recited a three-part negative behaviour of
which one part is observable in the `ambiguous` holdout cell.

One was closed by **producing evidence**: §4.10 above.

One was closed by **removing the recital from the independent claim entirely**.
It recited admission of accepted representations to a downstream trusted store.
No receipt exercises that, and the acceptance outcome has no consumer in the live
system outside its own module and unit tests. The available options were to build
an admission path so the limitation could be audited, or to move the limitation
to a dependent claim held for a continuation. We took the second. Building a
mechanism in order to make a claim testable inverts the relationship between the
system and the evidence, and a reader who later found that the admission path
existed only because a claim needed it would be right to distrust everything
else.

**The honest accounting of that trade.** Removing a limitation from an
independent claim makes it easier to infringe and harder to allow. Eleven of the
twenty-four registered amendments are therefore recorded as broadenings, five as
narrowings, and the direction of each is *derived from the operation* by a fixed
table rather than asserted by its author — a control that exists because an
earlier pass of this work recorded four broadenings as narrowings and the
mis-accounting hid where support was owed. Every removed recital is preserved in
a dependent claim and in the specification. None is discarded, and none is
described as a narrowing.

## 5. Limitations and threats to validity

1. **Controlled corpora still dominate.** §4.1 and §4.2 establish mechanism
   correctness on generated inputs. §4.1.1 adds a 28-pair retrospective natural
   Wikipedia regression, but it is one source family and does not establish that
   controlled change mixes or conflict patterns occur at these rates in practice.
2. **No threshold here is calibrated.** The identity bands (0.92 / 0.75 / 0.05)
   are conservative choices, not measured optima.
3. **Consumption completeness is unevidenced.** The build receipt cannot
   establish that the declared input set was complete. This is an open trust
   boundary and its long-term closure is not started.
3a. **Activation is contract-audited, not crash-tested.** §4.7 is a controlled
   unit-contract audit. Process crash mid-transaction, partial storage/network
   failure and multi-node concurrent publication are not demonstrated.
4. **Effect magnitude in §4.5 is uncertain.** The interval contains zero.
5. **External validity is narrow.** Natural-revision equivalence is now measured
   on one English-Wikipedia source family. Unseen domains, genuine amendment
   relationships in SEC/DART, unseen layouts and language diversity remain
   untested.
6. **Valid-time extraction is untested**, as distinct from valid-time reasoning.
7. **Identity scalability and identity equivalence are not simultaneously
   closed.** The sparse shadow removes the measured scaling ceiling, but its
   eight residual divergences are not permutations between tied halves: each is
   a **classification flip** between `NEW` and `AMBIGUOUS`, and all eight were
   measured path-dependent into the following revision. `AMBIGUOUS` carries no
   logical identifier, so a flip forks lineage at the next revision rather than
   resolving. The shadow is not promoted, and LEGACY remains the correctness
   baseline.
8. **Single-host timings.** All cost figures are from one CPU host; no
   distributed or contended measurement exists.
9. **One evaluator defect was found after unsealing** (§4.5) and corrected.
   Others may exist.
10. **The same-intelligence comparison is NOT_MEASURED.** §4.6's confirmatory run
    stopped at a pre-arm validity gate, so no arm was executed and no comparison
    between raw text, basic retrieval, enriched retrieval and a compiled world
    exists. This is not a null result and must not be read as one: nothing was
    measured that could have come out either way.
11. **The tie ceiling is operational, not derived.** The 10% exact-tie ceiling
    that stopped §4.6 was a predeclared operational validity threshold, not a
    theoretically derived constant. It was frozen 28 minutes before the holdout
    title list existed and was not relaxed afterwards, but we do not claim more
    for it than that.
12. **One holdout cohort, one source family, retrospective revisions.** §4.6 used
    English Wikipedia revision pairs separated by at least 1,095 days. There is no
    cross-source natural-revision validation, and the cohort is now spent: those
    titles are development data and cannot serve as a holdout again.
13. **Three reproducibility defects in §4.6's own instrumentation are disclosed
    rather than repaired in place.** The baseline gate's receipt carries a
    hard-coded `run_class: DEVELOPMENT_ONLY` although its recorded input digest is
    the holdout question set's (`W6-V8-GATE-01`); the endpoint sealer's
    arm-receipt filename map was written before the gates ran and silently omitted
    two receipts, corrected by an append-only supplement rather than by editing a
    sealing tool after its outcome was known (`W6-V8-SEAL-01`); and one
    intermediate version of the instrumentation provenance receipt was edited in
    place and is lost, so we claim neither full write-once history nor that every
    version was preserved.
14. **§4.6's first acquisition attempt failed on its own invocation**, not on
    science: 149 rate-limit retries followed by a deterministic path error that
    this repository had already diagnosed once. Its partial output is quarantined
    unexamined and was not reused; the second attempt ran over the identical
    sealed manifest, seal and cutoffs.
15. **Two receipts do not self-verify under the manifest's rule.** Both v8
    question-set receipts hash every field except the question list, by their
    generator's own convention, while the experiment manifest recomputes over the
    whole document. Each is internally consistent under its own rule and
    inconsistent under the other. The generator is frozen and digest-pinned and
    the holdout is closed, so it was not edited to resolve the mismatch after the
    fact.
16. **§4.10 is a contract audit, not a measurement.** It records what the live
    implementation does when an identity signal has no value. It does not measure
    how often that happens on real documents, and it asserts no completeness over
    the absence taxonomy: the four enumerated reasons are what this
    implementation distinguishes, and the receipt says so in a field rather than
    in prose.
17. **Four claim recitals are supported only by the implementation, and are held
    rather than filed.** Per-unit evidence-region binding, downstream
    trusted-store admission, the B-family evidence-region binding, and
    artifact-authored sensitivity declaration each exist in code with unit tests
    and are exercised by no receipt. They are recited in reserved dependent
    claims, kept out of the filing core, and reported in the element-support
    matrix at severity `DISCLOSED_RESERVED_NOT_IN_FILING_CORE`. None was made
    testable by building a mechanism for it.
18. **Three claims have no recoverable amendment history.** The claim set is
    deliberately untracked in version control, and the hash chain that replaces
    version history was pinned partway through the programme. It detects change
    from that point forward and reconstructs nothing before it, so for three
    dependent claims the record cannot distinguish "never amended" from "amended
    without an entry". They are reported UNDETERMINED, never "unamended".
19. **A withdrawn assertion survived in a registry field no detector scanned.**
    Three `paper_section` labels in the claim-evidence registry used the
    "atomic promotion" wording narrowed on 2026-08-19. The assertion audit scans
    surfaces — claim set, specification, manuscript, figures, tables — and a
    registry field that no surface printed was outside that set. It fired the
    moment Appendix A began rendering the field. Corrected; the general
    limitation stands, because every registry field is a candidate for future
    rendering and none of the others has been swept.
20. **One full-suite run recorded three failures that did not reproduce, and
    their identities were not captured.** The run used a summary-only traceback
    setting; a re-run under the same interpreter and the same collection
    (3,102 items) reported 3,034 passed, 68 skipped, zero failed. We record the
    discrepancy rather than resolving it, because we cannot: the failing test
    names are unrecoverable, and asserting a cause — contention, ordering, or
    anything else — would be a reconstruction, not a finding.

### 5.0.1 Selection and leakage

The 28 natural revision pairs are **every pair already stored** at the time of the
regression, not a sample drawn for it. No pair was selected, excluded or reordered
after any outcome was visible, so there is no selection leakage to inherit.

All-stored is not the same as randomly sampled. The pairs were originally
acquired for a different purpose, and whatever biases governed that acquisition
are inherited here unmeasured. We claim the absence of *outcome-dependent*
selection, which is a narrower and checkable property, and not
representativeness.

The 6,987-title manifest for the unrun retrieval experiment was likewise frozen
before any revision outcome was inspected, so a future retry inherits no
selection leakage either — a property that survives the acquisition having failed
twice.

## 5.1 Failure analysis

Every failure below is retained in full in the repository's failed-and-superseded
ledger. We summarise them here because a results section that omits them would
misrepresent how the system reached its current form.

**A structure-only revision left a stale artifact current.** A revision that
reordered units and changed no text produced no changed logical identifiers, so
the impact traversal started from an empty seed and a position-aggregated
artifact was carried forward as current. Equivalence was violated before the
promotion gate fired. We record this as a failure and not as a success of the
safety net: the gate holding is not evidence that the planner was right.

**A confirmatory evaluator was non-deterministic, and the defect was found after
unsealing.** The corrected figures are the citable ones and the correction is
disclosed as post-unsealing rather than presented as the original result. The
associated magnitude claim was withdrawn entirely: the risk-difference interval
contains zero, so the association survives and the effect size does not.

**A retrieval baseline was invalidated by leakage.** Questions built from the
retrieved passage produced recall near 1.0. A binding upper-bound gate caught it,
which is the argument for making ceilings binding rather than advisory.

**A falsification arm could not falsify.** Its weakening could not change any
answer by construction, so its zero divergences tested nothing. We have since hit
the same class of defect twice more — a permutation arm that could not diverge,
and an exactness corpus that could not reach the code paths under test — and the
rule now applied is that a null is uncitable until a positive control on the same
instrument separates.

**A faster identity policy was vetoed.** Discussed in §4.9. It remains
shadow-only.

**An exact replacement for it was slower than the baseline.** Also §4.9. We
report it because identifying *which* facet defeated this strategy is more useful
than another unexplained negative -- while noting that this identifies the
obstruction for the certificate we tested, not for the problem.

**We asserted, in our own specification, that an optimisation was exact when our
own experiment said otherwise.** The specification described the component
decomposition as exact; it is exact only against the already-restricted candidate
graph, and the measured divergence against the unrestricted problem is the reason
the policy is shadow-only. The claim text had been narrowed when the result
landed; the specification had not been swept in the same pass, and neither had a
figure caption. The rule now applied is that a narrowing updates claims,
specification, manuscript and figures together or it is not finished.

**We described a contract facet as preserved when nothing had measured it.** The
exact matcher's frozen contract lists next-revision lineage as an observable
facet; the harness stopped at the current revision, and a summary table said the
facet was preserved anyway. It turned out to be true when we measured it, and
that is not a defence: a sentence written from what a mechanism should do rather
than from what a receipt says is an overclaim whatever its truth value. Two
further facets were then found unmeasured and argued "strongly implied" -- the
same reasoning again -- so they were measured too. The sentence that then
announced all twelve facets covered was *itself* unsupported: it enumerated
eleven. The missing one, the assigned column, was the single per-row facet the
comparison function never read, and it had been called indirectly implied by the
logical identifier -- which does not follow, because that identifier is read from
the matched partner, so two prior units sharing one are indistinguishable through
it. We measured it directly by observing the resolver's own entry points rather
than reconstructing the assignment: zero divergences over nineteen cases, with
instrument liveness recorded alongside the null. Four occurrences of one habit,
the last of them inside the correction to the third.

**We destroyed one of our own receipts.** A tooling change intended to redirect an
output applied incompletely, and a re-run overwrote an earlier readiness receipt
while reporting it had written elsewhere. The path now holds a deterministic
regeneration whose hash necessarily differs from the lost original; both hashes
are pinned and the incident is retained. We report it because a rule against
destroying evidence is worth nothing if a violation of it can be tidied away.

**Our self-audit has not demonstrated convergence, and we decline to imply that
it has.** Three successive adversarial passes were run over this evidence base,
each begun after the previous one had been declared complete. Each found new
material defects: the first, two statements in our own specification that our own
receipts disprove; the second, a facet described as preserved that nothing had
measured; the third, a failure whose *cause* we had inferred from a disk
signature and never verified, a scope sentence that claimed twelve items while
listing eleven, and a "repository green" label attached to a subset of the test
suite. The rate of discovery did not fall.

The natural inference — that a fourth pass would find more — is one we accept
rather than argue against, and a fourth pass duly found three more: two
limitations we had withdrawn from claims on evidence still asserted in five other
places, a set of exactness results resting on a single corpus, and a suite of
test figures that described a different environment than the one they were run
against.

We have since frozen a stopping rule rather than continuing to decide after each
pass that enough had been done. It requires two consecutive rounds of eight
heterogeneous detectors — each with a stated blind spot, each carrying a positive
control that must fire in the same execution — with every prior finding closed.
**Zero such rounds have been completed.** The first was invalidated because its
human pass recorded no findings by an argument rather than a reading, and the
argument was one we constructed after noticing it would make the round clean. What we claim is narrower than an absence of defects:
every finding is recorded with the receipt that closed it, no finding was closed
by weakening the wording it contradicted, and the failed and superseded artifacts
are retained rather than repaired. A reader should treat the audit as a process
with a known nonzero yield, not as a certificate.

We think the shape of these defects is the transferable part. All of them are the
same act: a sentence written from what a mechanism *should* do rather than from
what a receipt *says*. Three were about our own mechanisms and one was about our
own failure, and the last of the four was produced by the fix for the third. The
mitigations that now exist — binding prose numbers to live receipts, requiring a
positive control before a null is citable, recording instrument liveness beside a
zero — were each added after that act had already happened once.

## 6. Reproducibility

Every result cites a receipt carrying its own sha256, the sha256 of the protocol
that governed it, the sha256 of the code modules it exercised, and its seed.
Superseded receipts are retained and are pinned by hash from the receipt that
supersedes them; none is edited or deleted. The claim–evidence matrix is
generated by a tool that re-reads every asserted number out of its receipt by
JSON pointer and refuses to emit if any disagree, and that checks the pinned
source modules still hash to what the measurement recorded.

At the time of writing that inventory is 24 experiments and 181 receipts, with
zero undeclared code-pin drift and **two receipts whose self-recorded hash does
not recompute**. We name them rather than round them away: both are v8
question-set receipts, and the cause is a convention mismatch, not tampering.
Their generator deliberately hashes every field *except* the question list, while
the manifest recomputes over the whole document minus the hash field. Each
receipt is internally consistent under its own rule and inconsistent under the
manifest's. The generator is a frozen, digest-pinned source and the holdout it
served is closed, so it is not edited to resolve this after the fact; the
discrepancy is disclosed here and in the reproducibility limitations instead. Two receipts *do* carry declared drift: the identity module was
reformatted after they were measured. We did not edit them to remove the
mismatch. They are listed in a drift register that states which is superseded and
which is claim-carrying, and the claim that cites the latter states the drift in
its own text; the manifest generator fails on undeclared drift only, so the gate
stays meaningful without rewriting history.

Three honest qualifications about this section, because a reproducibility claim
is the easiest one to overstate:

- The package digest is taken at **manifest time, not experiment time**. It is
  not a lockfile and we do not describe it as one.
- Of 24 experiments, exactly **one** has its original command line recoverable
  from a receipt. That number is about *history* and does not improve: the other
  23 invocations are gone. Separately, **eight** experiments now have an exact
  current-session command recorded, because their scripts take no arguments and
  the invocation is fully determined; the remainder carry reproduction
  *templates*. The execution index keeps these three categories apart rather than
  presenting a reconstructed command as history.
- Two artifacts in the repository are not reproducible in principle: a readiness
  receipt we destroyed with our own tooling, whose replacement is a deterministic
  regeneration with a necessarily different hash, and a partial acquisition
  directory left by a defect that our own diagnosis initially misattributed to a
  dying process. Both are retained with their status recorded.
- **The interpreter is part of a result, and we learned this the expensive way.**
  Every test figure this programme published was produced by an interpreter that
  is not the project environment. Under it, 24 tests failed in a way that reads as
  a product defect: the document-parsing sandbox launches its child process in
  isolated mode, which excludes user-installed packages, and the dependencies were
  installed there. The child could not import them, exited non-zero, and every
  downstream assertion saw an unfinished analysis task. Under the project
  environment the same isolated import succeeds. Nothing in the product was wrong,
  and nothing in the sandbox was relaxed to discover it — isolation is the
  boundary that keeps a hostile document away from the host, and trading it for a
  green number was not available. Build status is now emitted by a tool that
  records the interpreter and withholds the word *green* when it is not the
  project one.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 What the results support, at what strength

Three claims are carried by pre-registered one-shot evidence, and they are the
only three: selective recompilation reproduces an independent full rebuild
artifact-exactly on an adversarial controlled holdout; the activation gate
refuses a candidate state containing an artifact whose inputs cannot be verified;
and a joint agreement-and-stability gate over two extraction configurations is
*associated* with severe-error risk on a frozen 800-page real-document holdout.

The third is the weakest of the three and the most likely to be over-read. It is
an association at p = .032. The bootstrap interval for the risk difference
contains zero, so **no magnitude is established**, and no sentence in this paper
says the gate reduces severe errors by any amount.

Everything else is controlled-corpus mechanism evidence, contract-audit evidence
over live modules, or implementation only — and the paper's figure 6 draws the
four classes as four boxes rather than merging them into "validated".

### 7.2 What the negative results changed

This programme's negatives did more work than its positives, and each changed
something concrete rather than being noted and set aside.

The **structure-only staleness defect** is the reason the change classification
carries a structural kind at all. It was produced by an earlier version of this
system: a change that altered only document order produced no changed logical
identifiers, left the traversal seed empty, and carried a stale
position-aggregated artifact forward as CURRENT. The limitation that fixes it is
now the most distinctive element of the independent claim, and it exists because
the obvious design — classify by content — misses exactly the case with no
content delta.

The **identity path-dependence probe** vetoed a promotion. The candidate-indexed
matcher is roughly 47× faster and diverged on 8 of 33,600 decisions; all 8
diverged again in the following revision, and an `AMBIGUOUS` classification
carries no logical identifier, so each flip forks lineage. A speedup that
rewrites history is not a speedup, and the policy remains shadow-only.

The **exact certified sparse matcher** removed the last route around that: it
preserved the full contract and ran at 0.35–0.48× the dense baseline. We report
that as a property of one certification strategy on one corpus. It bounds that
strategy and it bounds nothing about the problem: a different certificate for the
runner-up, or a formulation that does not require preserving it, is untested here
and is excluded by nothing we measured.

The **governance filter ablation** is the one that cost claim scope. Permission,
temporal-validity and applicability filters turned out to be redundant for the
verdict whenever a compliant alternative exists, and the sole refusal only when
none does. Four ranking elements were not measurable at all by that instrument.
The claim may not be prosecuted as though each filter were independently
necessary, and it is not.

The **gate-ordering ablation** narrowed an element to an order-invariant
conjunction, and the specification took a year of drafting inertia to catch up:
two sentences still asserted that the order was material until 2026-08-20.

### 7.3 The comparative endpoint, and why its absence is load-bearing

The experiment this programme most wanted was the simplest to state: give one
fixed model a compiled world versus raw text versus basic retrieval, and see
whether the compiled world answers revision-sensitive questions better.

It did not run. The confirmatory holdout failed a predeclared pre-arm
retrieval-validity criterion — exact ties at 19/166 = 11.45% against a 10%
ceiling frozen six hours before the question set existed — and the arms were not
executed. **The comparative endpoint is NOT_MEASURED**, which is a different
statement from a comparison that ran and came out flat. Measured inference cost:
$0.00.

The honest consequence is that this paper establishes *mechanism* properties of a
knowledge compiler and establishes **nothing** about whether a model answers
better with one. A reader who wants that number will not find it here, and the
cohort that would have produced it is spent: it is development data now, and a
future comparative result requires a fresh untouched cohort at real cost.

We report the stop rather than the workaround because a gate honoured only when
the excess feels small is not a gate. The excess here was three questions.

### 7.4 What we would tell someone building this

Three things, in the order they cost us most.

**Separate operational failure from semantic failure, and separate absence from
zero.** Both are the same discipline applied at different layers: a signal with no
value is not a signal scoring zero, and a pod that died is not a model that was
wrong. Every place this programme collapsed the two, it produced a number that
looked like evidence and was not.

**Build the detector's positive control before the detector.** Every audit in
this repository runs a probe through its own classifier in the same execution,
because a guard that cannot fire reads identically to a clean result. We learned
this by shipping guards that could not fire.

**Assume a withdrawn claim survives somewhere.** Three separate incidents (P19,
P22, P29) found a retracted proposition alive in a document that a sweep had
declared clean, each time in a form the previous detector was not built for.
Nothing here establishes that the current detectors catch the next one, and the
useful posture is to expect the fourth rather than to declare the class closed.

### 7.5 Threats we cannot design around

The controlled corpora still dominate. The natural-revision regression is 28
pairs from one source family, acquired for another purpose, and it is *every pair
already stored* rather than a sample — which buys the absence of
outcome-dependent selection and buys nothing about representativeness. No
threshold in this system is calibrated; the identity bands are conservative
choices, not measured optima, and `CalibrationTable.calibrated` is `False` and
refuses to be set true without naming a corpus.

And the build receipt cannot establish that a builder's declared input set was
complete. That is a boundary of the mechanism rather than a gap in the
measurement: a wrong input set can be sealed perfectly, and the only guarantee
available is fail-closed or degraded treatment of untracked builders.

---

## 8. Conclusion

We treated document processing as compilation over a mutable world and measured
what that costs.

Selective recompilation reproduced an independent full rebuild artifact-exactly
on a pre-registered controlled holdout, and on all 28 natural revision pairs we
had already stored — the latter retrospective, single-source-family, and
explicitly not a generalisation to arbitrary documents. The verification gate
refuses a candidate state containing an artifact whose inputs cannot be verified,
and treats unverifiable as distinct from passing; a permutation study showed its
acceptance decision is invariant to check ordering, so we narrowed the
corresponding claim rather than defend an ordering we could not show mattered.

The results we expect to be most useful are the negative ones. A candidate-
bucketing identity policy is roughly forty-seven times faster and produces a
different history, so it is vetoed. An exact replacement preserving the full
observable contract is slower than the dense baseline it was meant to accelerate,
because preserving which unit would have been the *runner-up* requires retaining
essentially every candidate when two revisions have similar unit counts.
Exclusivity is what makes inferred identity trustworthy, and it is also what
makes it expensive; we can now say which facet closes the door rather than
reporting an unexplained failure.

Three questions stay open and we mark them as open rather than answered: whether
exact acceleration is achievable under a cheaper runner-up certificate; whether
these results hold on a second source family, for which no corpus with explicit
amendment relationships was available to us; and whether the thresholds
throughout are calibrated, which they are not and which no result here claims.
